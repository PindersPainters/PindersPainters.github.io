Preparing Your Cornwall Home for Winter Painting

6 November 2026 · 4 min read

PP
Pinder's Painters6 November 2026 · 4 min read · Seasonal Advice
Cornwall winters are wet, salty and hard on paintwork. A bit of preparation before the worst weather arrives protects your home and saves you from bigger repairs in spring.

Why winter prep matters here

Coastal air, driving rain and temperature swings make peeling, blistering and damp staining more likely — especially on exposed elevations and timber. Sorting weak spots now means you are not chasing leaks and flaking paint when the weather turns properly nasty.

Check the outside first

Walk the property on a dry day and look for:

  • Cracked or missing sealant around windows and doors
  • Flaking or chalky paint on render, timber or metalwork
  • Soft or rotten timber on fascias, bargeboards and window sills
  • Areas where water sits or runs behind gutters

Small defects left until spring often become larger, more expensive jobs.

Prep beats a quick coat

Good painting in Cornwall is mostly preparation. Wash down dirty or algae-stained surfaces, scrape loose paint, fill and sand, and let timber dry thoroughly before priming. On damp or poorly prepared walls, even an expensive topcoat will fail early.

Indoors: damp, mould and busy rooms

Inside, autumn and winter bring condensation. Wipe down affected areas, fix the cause where you can (ventilation, heating patterns), and only then repaint with a suitable finish. Hallways, kitchens and rental voids also take a battering — a tough, wipeable finish lasts longer than a soft emulsion in high-traffic spaces.

When to call a professional

If you have large areas of failed paint, suspected damp, rotten timber, or a commercial or rental property that needs turning around quickly, a local decorator who knows Cornish weather will get a longer-lasting result than a rushed DIY coat.
