The Complete Guide to Exterior Painting in Cornwall

5 February 2026 · 6 min read

PP
Pinder's Painters5 February 2026 · 6 min read · Exterior Painting
Exterior painting in Cornwall is a different proposition to almost anywhere else in the UK. Atlantic gales, relentless rain, salt spray, and long damp winters put any paintwork under serious stress. This guide covers how often to repaint, which products hold up, and what a proper job involves.

Why Cornwall's Climate Is So Demanding on Exterior Paint

The Cornish coastline receives some of the highest rainfall in England, combined with strong westerly winds that drive moisture into every crack and crevice. Properties within a few miles of the sea face salt-laden air — a persistent cause of paint failure. Salt draws moisture in, degrades adhesion, and accelerates peeling on poorly prepared surfaces.

Exterior paintwork in Cornwall simply doesn't last as long as it would in, say, London or the Midlands. The answer is choosing the right products, preparing surfaces thoroughly, and repainting on a sensible schedule.

How Often Should You Repaint Your Cornwall Home's Exterior?

Surface typeTypical intervalKey watch-out
Rendered walls (masonry paint)5–8 yearsCracking around windows; salt crystallisation
Timber cladding / boarding3–5 yearsPaint flaking at joints where moisture enters
Soffits and fascia boards4–6 yearsPeeling at joins; bird or insect damage
Window frames (painted timber)3–5 yearsSills deteriorate fastest — check there first
Gates, railings & metalwork4–7 yearsRust spotting under paint in coastal areas

Choosing the Right Exterior Paint for Cornwall

  • High vapour permeability — lets moisture in walls breathe out rather than trapping it. Particularly important on older stone and cob properties common across Cornwall.
  • Algae and mould resistance — Cornwall's damp air encourages green algae on north-facing walls. Choose a masonry paint with built-in biocide.
  • Flexibility — rendered walls expand and contract with temperature changes. Flexible masonry paints accommodate this without cracking.
  • UV resistance — cheaper paints fade visibly within a couple of seasons on south-facing elevations.
Coastal Cornwall Tip

Within roughly 3–5 miles of the coast, standard masonry paint formulations can fail faster than manufacturers' claims. Ask specifically about salt-resistant or coastal-rated products — several major brands offer these as distinct lines, not just marketing terms.

What a Professional Exterior Paint Job Involves

  1. Surface inspection Walk the full perimeter and note any cracking, failed render, rot in timber, rust in metalwork, or evidence of damp. These need addressing before any paint goes on.
  2. Cleaning and preparation Pressure washing to remove loose paint, algae, lichen, salt deposits, and dirt. Any mould or algae areas get a biocide wash. This step is often skipped — it's the main reason cheaper exterior jobs fail.
  3. Repairs Fill cracks in render, treat rust on metalwork, address rot in timber before priming.
  4. Priming Bare masonry, repaired areas, and previously unpainted surfaces all get appropriate primer for better adhesion.
  5. Two coats of topcoat Applied at the correct coverage rate — not thinned down to go further. Undercoating the paint application is a false economy that halves the finish life.
  6. Trims and details Soffits, fascia, window frames, sills, and doors finished with the right products for each surface type.

Summary

Cornwall's exterior painting needs a different approach to elsewhere in the UK — better products, more thorough preparation, and a realistic maintenance schedule.
